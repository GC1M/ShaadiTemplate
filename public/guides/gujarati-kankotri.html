How a Gujarati kankotri is laid out

Guide · By Ashok Lalakiya · Updated November 2026

The kankotri (કંકોત્રી) is the Gujarati wedding invitation. In many families the first printed card is placed before Ganesha or the family deity before any are sent out, and the rest go to relatives by hand. A kankotri follows a fairly settled order, and relatives notice when a section is missing. This guide goes through a typical card from top to bottom, with the Gujarati terms you will see and what belongs in each part.

1. Invocation and shlok

The card opens with ॥ શ્રી ગણેશાય નમઃ ॥, often followed by the shlok:

વક્રતુંડ મહાકાય સૂર્યકોટિ સમપ્રભ
નિર્વિઘ્નં કુરુ મે દેવ સર્વકાર્યેષુ સર્વદા

Families who follow Pushtimarg or another sampradaya often add or substitute a line to Shrinathji or their kuldevi. Keep this section short and centred; it sets the tone for the whole card.

2. The letter (પરિણયોત્સવ)

The heart of the kankotri is a short formal letter addressed to the guest, usually beginning સ્નેહી સ્વજન શ્રી ("dear relative"). It announces that, by the grace of the family deity, the wedding of the daughter or son has been fixed, gives both names with their parents, the date, and asks the guest to come with their whole family to bless the couple. A typical closing is સહકુટુંબ પધારશોજી, "please come with your family".

Names carry traditional prefixes. The bride and groom are written as ચિ. (chiranjeevi, "may you live long"). Married women are અ.સૌ. (akhand saubhagyavati), and men are શ્રી, with ભાઈ or બેન added to first names: અ.સૌ. સુનીતાબેન તથા શ્રી રાજેશભાઈ પટેલની સુપુત્રી ચિ. કૃપા.

3. માંગલિક અવસરો: the functions before the wedding

Next comes a list of the rituals and celebrations in the days before. Each gets its own small block with the date, day, time, the family member performing it and the place. Common entries are:

  • ગણેશ સ્થાપન, installing Ganesha at home to begin the wedding rituals.
  • મંડપ મુહૂર્ત, the auspicious start of building the wedding mandap, often performed by a married couple from the family.
  • ગ્રહ શાંતિ, a puja for the planets, usually a day or two before the wedding.
  • પીઠી, the turmeric ceremony (Haldi).
  • મામેરું, when the bride's maternal uncle's family brings gifts.
  • રાસ ગરબા or સંગીત સંધ્યા, the evening of music and dance.

Write the day as well as the date, in the form તા. ૨૦/૧૧/૨૦૨૭ ને શનિવાર. Gujarati numerals are traditional on a fully Gujarati card; if half your guests read only English digits, it is fine to use those instead, but do not mix the two on one card.

4. The wedding day (સોનેરી અવસરો)

The wedding day itself gets a separate, more prominent section. On a bride's family card it usually lists:

  • જાન આગમન, the arrival of the groom's procession, with the time.
  • હસ્તમેળાપ, the joining of hands, the central moment of the ceremony.
  • ભોજન સમારંભ, the meal, often with a note such as આપશ્રી સજોડે પધારશોજી ("please come with your spouse") so guests know who is included.
  • લગ્ન સ્થળ, the venue and full address.

On the groom's family card the same section often lists the departure of the jaan from home instead of its arrival.

5. ટહુકો: the children's line

The ટહુકો (tahuko, literally a bird's call) is a short rhyme written as if from the young children of the family, inviting guests to "our aunt's wedding" or "our uncle's wedding" and signed with the children's names. It is the most personal part of the card and many families write a fresh one each time. For example:

ઢોલ વાગે, શરણાઈ ગુંજે, આંગણે અવસર આવ્યો,
માસીના લગ્નમાં વહેલા વહેલા પધારજો...
- આરવ, દીયા, કબીર, માયરા

6. Family names: દર્શનાભિલાષી and નિમંત્રક

The last section lists the family. દર્શનાભિલાષી ("eager to see you") names the wider family who look forward to welcoming the guest, often three generations of men and women. નિમંત્રક names the people formally sending the invitation, usually the father and his brothers, with a phone number. Some cards also include લગ્નોત્સુક, the cousins and siblings "eager for the wedding".

This is where most mistakes happen, because it has the most names. Ask one person from each branch of the family to check their branch's spellings and order before you print.

Practical tips

  • Fonts. Use a proper Gujarati typeface. Gujarati rendered in a font that lacks the script shows broken conjuncts, and elders will spot it immediately.
  • Typing. If you do not have a Gujarati keyboard, phonetic typing lets you type krupa and get કૃપા. Check each word, because names can convert to an unexpected spelling.
  • Colour. Red, maroon, saffron and gold are the classic kankotri colours. Bandhani and patola patterns are popular, and many families send a Pithi card and a garba night card alongside the kankotri.
  • Printed and digital. Many families now print a smaller run for elders and the family deity, and send the same card as an image on WhatsApp to everyone else. See printing and sharing your card.
