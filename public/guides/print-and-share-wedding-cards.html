Printing and sharing your wedding card

Guide · By Ashok Lalakiya · Updated November 2026

Most families now need their invitation in two forms: a printed card for elders, the family deity and guests who expect one by hand, and a digital version for everyone on WhatsApp. The two have different requirements. A card that looks perfect on a phone can print soft and blurry, and a print file is too large and the wrong shape for a phone screen. This guide covers both.

Two formats, two shapes

Phone format is tall and narrow, in a 9:16 ratio, the same shape as a phone screen and a WhatsApp status. On ShaadiTemplate it downloads as a 1080 by 1920 pixel image per page. Guests can read it without zooming, and it fills the screen when opened.

Print format is 5 by 7 inches (about 127 by 178 mm), in a 5:7 ratio. It is one of the most common invitation sizes, it fits standard envelopes, and most local presses can cut it without a custom die. On ShaadiTemplate the print PDF is rendered at 1800 by 2520 pixels per page, roughly 360 dots per inch.

Do not print the phone version or send the print version on WhatsApp. Switch the format in the editor before you download; the layout adjusts to the new shape.

Getting it printed

What resolution is enough?

Printers usually ask for 300 dots per inch at the final size. Below about 200, fine text and thin gold borders start to look fuzzy. A 5 by 7 inch card needs at least 1500 by 2100 pixels to hit 300 dpi, which is why the print PDF is larger than that. If a press says your file is "low resolution", check that you sent the print PDF and not a screenshot or a WhatsApp-forwarded copy, since WhatsApp compresses images.

What to tell the press

  • Finished size: 5 x 7 inches.
  • Pages: how many pages the PDF has, and whether they should be printed single-sided cards, double-sided, or folded. A three-page PDF can become a cover card plus a double-sided insert.
  • Paper: for a flat card, 300 to 350 gsm art card or textured paper feels substantial. Thinner paper (170 to 250 gsm) works for inserts.
  • Finish: matte is easier to read and write on; gloss makes colours richer but shows fingerprints. Real gold foil is a separate process and needs a separate file from the press; the gold in a PDF prints as a yellow-brown colour, not metal.
  • Proof: always ask for one printed proof before the full run. Screens show colours brighter than paper, especially deep maroons and greens.

Colours on paper

Phone and laptop screens glow; paper only reflects. Expect printed colours to come out a little darker and less saturated than on screen. Dark backgrounds such as maroon and royal blue look rich but can lose detail in the shadows, so check the proof under normal room light, not next to a window. If text on a dark background looks thin in print, increase the text size in the editor slightly before downloading again.

How many to print

Count households, not people: one card per family. Add 10 to 15 percent for last-minute additions, cards for the temple and elders, and a few to keep. Gujarati and Marathi families often need more than they first estimate once the extended family list is complete, so finish the guest list before you place the order.

Sharing on WhatsApp

Keep it sharp

WhatsApp compresses photos sent the normal way, which softens small text. Two ways to avoid this:

  • Send the image as a document (attach, then Document). The guest receives the original file. The downside is that it shows as a file rather than a preview.
  • Send the normal way but use the phone format, which is designed at a size that survives compression well. For most guests this is the better choice.

On phones that support it, the Share button in the ShaadiTemplate editor sends the finished image straight to WhatsApp or any other app, without saving it to the gallery first.

Multi-page invitations

If your invitation has several pages (a cover, the wedding details, the list of functions), send them in order in one message so they appear together. Alternatively, send the PDF of the phone format: one file, every page in order, and it opens in WhatsApp's built-in viewer.

One link for every function

If you are sending cards for several functions, a free wedding website keeps them in one place: guests open a single link to see each event with its time and venue, add it to their calendar, find the map and reply. Send the website link with the card image, so the card does the welcoming and the website answers the questions.

Broadcast lists, not groups

A WhatsApp broadcast list sends the invitation to each person individually, so it arrives as a personal message rather than in a crowded group. Recipients must have your number saved for broadcasts to reach them. For elders, a short personal line before the card ("Kaka, please come with the whole family") goes a long way.

A simple checklist

  1. Finish the wording and have both families check every name and date.
  2. Download the print PDF and get one proof from the press.
  3. Fix anything the proof shows, then place the full print order.
  4. Switch to the phone format and download the WhatsApp version from the same, corrected text.
  5. Send printed cards to elders and the temple first, then the digital version to everyone else.
