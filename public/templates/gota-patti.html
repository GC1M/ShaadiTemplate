Gota Patti Rani Pink: Hindi wedding card

About this design

A rani pink silk card, darker at the edges. Across the top and bottom runs a wide rose-gold gota ribbon, woven in a fine twill with a band of cut gota leaves and a scalloped lace edge, the kind stitched to the border of a Rajasthani lehenga or odhni. From all four corners rise large sprays of gota leaves in rose gold, dotted with small gold flowers.

All six pages share the same lace and corner sprays, so the set looks like one piece. Titles are in rose gold and the text is in soft white, with the bride and groom in rose gold on the cover. The cover has a small Om roundel, the emblem used on this site for Lord Ganesha cards, between the Ganesha shloka and the title.

It suits Hindi-speaking families, especially from Rajasthan and North India, who want a rich festive wedding card that still reads cleanly on a phone. For the evening before, Pichhwai Lotus Pond is a Hindi sangeet card, and Choodi Bangle Save the Date is a Hindi save the date with the same Ganesha line.

Sample wording on this card

The cover opens with ॥ श्री गणेशाय नमः ॥ and the shloka वक्रतुण्ड महाकाय, then शुभ विवाह and a line from the bride's parents inviting you सपरिवार. The bride and groom are written with चि., each with their parents' names, joined by संग. Below come the date, the baraat and हस्त मिलाप times, a sample palace venue in Udaipur and the निवेदक (hosts) with a phone number.

The other five pages are मांगलिक अवसर (mandap muhurat, a garba evening and a short बाल मनुहार verse from the children), सुनहरे पल (baraat, hast milap, dinner and venue), परिणयोत्सव (the letter), स्नेह बंधन (the family names, with ननिहाल पक्ष and प्यारी बेटियाँ) and डिजिटल आमंत्रण (the closing message asking guests to reply OK).

Replace every name, date and address with your own before you share it. The card can also be switched to English, Gujarati, Marathi, Punjabi or Tamil in the editor, with the same pages and artwork.
