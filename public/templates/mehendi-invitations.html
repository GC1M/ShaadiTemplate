Mehendi invitations

The Mehendi is an afternoon or evening gathering where the bride, and often every woman in the family, has henna applied. Guests come and go over several hours, so give a start time with 'onwards' rather than a fixed slot. Mention music or food if there will be any, and note if it is a women-only event.

Like Haldi cards, Mehendi cards are mostly shared digitally. Choose a design that matches the colours of the day: greens and teals sit well beside henna.

Four new English mehndi cards come first: a green marigold arch with the couple on a carved bench, rani pink and emerald damask cards with a henna bride, and a two-page garden terrace card.

Every design below opens in the editor with sample wording for this ceremony. You can switch any card to English, Hindi, Marathi, Gujarati, Punjabi or Tamil once it is open.

Marigold Arch Mehndi

English · Floral, no deity

A deep green jharokha arch with a thin gold outline stands on cream paper, hung with strings of orange and yellow marigolds. At the bottom an illustrated couple in matching green kurta and lehenga sit on a carved wooden bench with polka-dot cushions, the bride showing her henna, between bunches of white jasmine and peonies.

Rani Damask Mehndi

English · Floral, no deity

A rani pink damask of star medallions on deep maroon, framed by a thin saffron border. At the bottom a bride in a pink lehenga sits on silk cushions and hides her face behind hennaed hands, between two brass shehnai-style trumpets with green tassels.
