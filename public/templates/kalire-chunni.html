About this design

A pale champagne card, finely dotted with gold sitara like a bridal chunni, with a row of gold kalire, the dangling bridal ornaments tied to a Punjabi bride's chooda, hanging from a gold kara that runs across the top edge. Each one is a stack of small gold domes fringed with tiny pearls, ending in a gold leaf drop with a red stone, and they hang at different lengths so the row looks like it is swaying. Across the bottom is the chooda itself, seen side-on along the arm: a long row of solid lac bangles overlapping in perspective, red in sets split by ivory, with a pair of gold kundan bangles set with red and white stones in the middle.

The text starts with ੴ ਸਤਿ ਨਾਮੁ and a round Ek Onkar emblem in maroon, then ਮੰਗਣੀ ਦੀ ਰਸਮ in large maroon Gurmukhi. The bride and groom's names are in the same maroon with their parents written as Sardarni and Sardar, joined by ਸੰਗ. The date, an evening time, the gurdwara and the hosts follow in dark grey.

It suits Sikh and Punjabi families announcing a mangni, roka or ring ceremony. The kalire and chooda are the bride's own wedding jewellery, so the card hints at what comes next without showing the couple. Harmandir Gold Leaf carries the same Ek Onkar emblem for the Anand Karaj, and Phulkari Bagh Mehndi covers the mehndi night in Punjabi.
